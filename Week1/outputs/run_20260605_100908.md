Q1: A trader uses a moving average crossover strategy to buy stocks when the short-term moving average crosses above the long-term moving average. However, this strategy has resulted in significant drawdowns during periods of high volatility. What is the primary issue with this strategy?

Topic: Trading Logic and Risk Management

Answer: The primary issue with this strategy is that it does not account for the impact of high volatility on the moving averages. During periods of high volatility, the short-term and long-term moving averages may not accurately reflect the underlying trend, leading to false signals and increased drawdowns.

Q2: A portfolio manager wants to calculate the Sharpe ratio for a portfolio with an average return of 10% per annum and a standard deviation of 15%. However, the portfolio also has a non-normal distribution due to the presence of outliers. What is the best approach to estimate the standard deviation in this case?

Topic: Statistical Analysis and Risk Measurement

Answer: The best approach to estimate the standard deviation in this case would be to use the median absolute deviation (MAD) instead of the sample standard deviation. This is because the MAD is a more robust measure of dispersion that can handle outliers, providing a more accurate estimate of the portfolio's risk.

Q3: A quant researcher wants to develop a trading strategy based on the relative strength index (RSI). The RSI is calculated as the ratio of the average gain to the average loss over a given period. However, the researcher notices that the RSI values are not always between 0 and 100. What is the correct interpretation of the RSI in this case?

Topic: Technical Analysis and Trading Logic

Answer: In this case, the RSI values should be interpreted as a percentage value, where 0 represents a loss of 100% and 100 represents a gain of 100%. This means that an RSI value of 50 would indicate a neutral state, while an RSI value above 70 would indicate overbought conditions and a value below 30 would indicate oversold conditions.
