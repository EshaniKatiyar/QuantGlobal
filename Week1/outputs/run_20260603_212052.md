Q[2]: A portfolio manager wants to optimize the allocation of her equity portfolio by minimizing its value-at-risk (VaR). She has three asset classes: stocks, bonds, and commodities. The historical VaR for each class is as follows:
- Stocks: 5%
- Bonds: 3%
- Commodities: 8%

The portfolio manager wants to allocate her portfolio such that the overall VaR is 4%. What is the optimal allocation of her portfolio among these three asset classes?

Topic: Portfolio Optimization

Answer: To solve this problem, we can use a linear programming approach. Let x1, x2, and x3 be the weights allocated to stocks, bonds, and commodities respectively. We want to minimize the overall VaR subject to the constraint that the overall VaR is 4%. The optimization problem can be formulated as follows:
Minimize 0.04x1 + 0.03x2 + 0.08x3
Subject to x1 + x2 + x3 = 1 (portfolio allocation constraint)
x1, x2, and x3 ≥ 0 (non-negativity constraints)

Solving this optimization problem using linear programming techniques, we get the optimal weights as:
x1 = 0.33
x2 = 0.33
x3 = 0.34

Therefore, the optimal allocation of the portfolio is 33% in stocks, 33% in bonds, and 34% in commodities.
