Q001: 
A trader observes that the daily returns for a stock are normally distributed with a mean of 0.05 and a standard deviation of 0.1. If the trader wants to buy the stock if its return is greater than 0.06, what is the probability that this event will occur?

Topic: Probability and Statistics

Answer: 
The probability that the event will occur can be calculated using the z-score formula:

z = (X - μ) / σ
where X is the desired return (0.06), μ is the mean (0.05), and σ is the standard deviation (0.1).

First, calculate the z-score:
z = (0.06 - 0.05) / 0.1 = 0.5

Then, use a standard normal distribution table or calculator to find the probability that z > 0.5.
P(z > 0.5) = 1 - P(z ≤ 0.5) ≈ 1 - 0.6915 ≈ 0.3085

Therefore, the probability that the event will occur is approximately 0.3085 or 30.85%.

Q002: 
Suppose we have a trading strategy that involves buying a stock when its price rises by more than 10% in a single day and selling it when its price falls by less than 5%. The daily returns for this strategy are normally distributed with a mean of 0.03 and a standard deviation of 0.15. What is the probability that the strategy will result in a profit on any given trading day?

Topic: Probability and Statistics

Answer: 
To calculate the probability of a profit, we need to find the probability that the daily return is greater than -0.05 (i.e., the price rises by more than 10% or falls by less than 5%).

First, calculate the z-score for a return of -0.05:
z = (-0.05 - 0.03) / 0.15 ≈ -0.5333

Then, use a standard normal distribution table or calculator to find the probability that z > -0.5333.
P(z > -0.5333) = P(z < 0.5333) ≈ 0.7019 (since the distribution is symmetric)

Therefore, the probability that the strategy will result in a profit on any given trading day is approximately 70.19%.
