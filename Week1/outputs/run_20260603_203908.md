Q001: A stock price follows a geometric Brownian motion with drift μ = 0.05 and volatility σ = 0.2. If the initial stock price is $100, what is the probability that the stock price will be above $120 in one year?

Topic: Geometric Brownian Motion

Answer: The probability that the stock price will be above $120 in one year can be calculated using the cumulative distribution function (CDF) of the lognormal distribution. Let X be a random variable representing the stock price at time t = 1, then:

X ~ Lognormal(μ, σ^2)

The CDF of the lognormal distribution is given by:

F(x) = P(X ≤ x) = Φ((ln(x) - μ) / σ)

where Φ is the standard normal CDF.

We want to find P(X > 120), so we need to find 1 - F(120):

P(X > 120) = 1 - Φ((ln(120) - 0.05) / 0.2)
= 1 - Φ(-0.35)
≈ 0.626

Therefore, the probability that the stock price will be above $120 in one year is approximately 62.6%.
